The Coincidence Machine

Three honest pictures of the golden-ratio Standard Model audit — drawn from devin's 2026-09-30 numbers, in my own hand. The audit's verdict, in one line: the hits are real arithmetic, and they are exactly what the null hypothesis predicts.

Scene 1 — The 1400× zoom. The lambda formula matches the central value to about six decimal places — real arithmetic, genuinely pretty. But the measurement's own uncertainty is roughly 1400× wider than the gap between formula and central value (audit §A). Drag the magnifier: the "hit" is a gold sliver swimming inside a sea of noise. A bullseye painted on the ocean is still a bullseye — it just doesn't mean the ocean aimed.

Scene 2 — The search that finds. The audit tried 603,000 unique formulas across 112 grammar cells — and found coincidences sharper than the original claim (1.6e-7 vs 4.3e-6). The family-wide null says: with that many tries, expect 4.5 hits per table; the chance of at least one hit is effectively 1.0 (audit §B). Finding a hit isn't evidence — it's the forecast. Even the honest failure is on the books: α⁻¹ got 22,000 formulas and its nearest miss was 0.54.

Scene 3 — The running. Run the claims up in energy and they melt: the lambda residual slides from −2.5% at Mt to −50% at 10 TeV; sin²θW climbs from +1.3% to +10.3%. One curve — √(4λ/yt²) — crosses its phi-claim near ~680 GeV on the way down. But grad(F)·β is nonzero at every scale: none of these is an RG invariant (audit §C). A crossing is also a coincidence. Curves are schematic — endpoints from the audit, shapes illustrative.
