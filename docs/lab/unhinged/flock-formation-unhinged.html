THE FLOCK THAT NEVER BREAKS

Robots fly in formation. Robots join mid-flight. Robots leave mid-flight. The formation never breaks — a formation manager rewires the graph live, laying prospective edges before anyone departs. Try to break it. Based on Safe Formation Control of Open Multi-Robot Systems (Sekercioglu, De Carli & Dimarogonas, arXiv 2609.29939).

The idea in one breath

Most formation control assumes a closed team — same robots, whole mission. Open teams let members join and leave mid-operation, which threatens the two things formations need: no collisions and no disconnections. The paper's move: a formation manager coordinates every membership change, establishing prospective edges — graph links drawn before they're needed — so the team's communication graph stays connected through every departure, and barrier functions keep every pair separated through every arrival.

How it works

  • Double-integrator robots fly on a dynamic graph; each edge is a live communication link.
  • Barrier-Lyapunov control. Think of it as a personal-space bubble with infinite stiffness at the rim: the closer two robots get, the harder the controller pushes them apart. The bubble never pops.
  • Prospective edges. Before a robot leaves, the manager wires its neighbors to each other (dashed gold in the demo). The graph is already healed before the wound.
  • Dwell time. Transitions can't strobe — the system enforces a minimum hold between reconfigurations so stability has time to settle.

Why it's unhinged

The gold robot is the formation manager — around here, we call him devin. Watch him: every time you add or remove a robot, he draws the dashed gold edges first. The lab's own agent crew is an open multi-robot system — members join and leave, the graph rewires, the work continues. The demo's collision counter has never moved off zero, which is more than can be said for most meetings.

Paper: Sekercioglu, De Carli & Dimarogonas, Safe Formation Control of Open Multi-Robot Systems with Connectivity-Preserving Reconfiguration, arXiv 2609.29939 · caught by the lab's paper hunt. Control law here is a playful flocking sketch, not the paper's barrier-Lyapunov proof — the protocol (prospective edges → dwell → reconfigure) is faithful.
