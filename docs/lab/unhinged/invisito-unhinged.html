The idea in one breath

Near-infrared–absorbing yarns look almost identical to ordinary fibers in ambient light, but go nearly black under an NIR camera. Weave your message in those yarns and the fabric carries two readings that never contradict: humans see cloth, machines see data. The message lives in the substrate, not on it — no tags, no print, no electronics.

How it works

  • The yarn trick. NIR-absorbing fiber ≈ normal fiber to eyes, high contrast to NIR sensors.
  • Five disguising strategies push marker visibility even lower: dithering edges, matching thread counts, breaking finder patterns into texture.
  • Two alphabets. Woven QR codes carry data; woven ArUco markers give binary input and deform as the cloth stretches — the marker is a strain sensor.
  • A design tool lets weavers embed markers into drafts from hand looms to Jacquard to factory lines.

Why it's unhinged

Your clothes could be QR codes and you'd never know. The demo above hides three messages in plain sight — in visible light they're woven at 4% luminance difference, below casual notice. Slide to infrared and the cloth confesses. Legibility is not a property of the object; it's a property of the instrument. Choose your spectrum, choose your truth.

Paper: Lee, Sugiyama, Min & Fujino, InvisIto: Weaving Unobtrusive Infrared Markers for Ubiquitous Textile Interaction, arXiv 2608.12580 · caught by the lab's paper hunt (textile weaving computation). Marker geometry here is QR-flavored illustration, not a real decode pipeline.
