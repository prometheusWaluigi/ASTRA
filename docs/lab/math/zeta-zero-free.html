The Zero-Free Frontier

Where the Riemann zeta function is certified to have no zeros — the fences mathematicians have built since 1896, and the bold, unverified claim that just redrew the map.

The idea

i.A certified no-zeros zone

The Riemann zeta function ζ(s) has infinitely many zeros. The dull ones sit on the negative real axis — at −2, −4, −6, … — the trivial zeros, forced by the functional equation. The interesting ones, the nontrivial zeros, all lie somewhere inside the critical strip, 0 < σ < 1, where σ = Re(s).

A zero-free region is a theorem shaped like a fence: it certifies that some part of the strip contains no zeros at all. Every nontrivial zero must live to the left of the fence. The further left the fence is pushed, the smaller the territory in which zeros are allowed to hide.

The stakes

ii.Why march toward the critical line?

The primes are counted by ψ(x), and the prime number theorem says ψ(x) is close to x. How close? The error is dictated by the zeros: each zero ρ = β + iγ contributes a wave of size about xβ. Cap how far right the zeros can reach, and the error shrinks.

Riemann Hypothesis (all zeros on σ = ½)  ⇒  error ≈ √x · log²x
Vinogradov–Korobov fence (1958, still the shape record)  ⇒  error ≈ x · exp(−c (log x)3/5 / (log log x)1/5)

That 1958 error term is barely below x itself — the fence hugs σ = 1 so tightly that it has barely budged in 68 years. A uniform wall at σ = 11/12 would collapse the error to roughly x11/12: the first certified strip of fixed, t-independent width — a quasi-Riemann Hypothesis. That is why 11/12 matters. Not because the number is magic, but because a wall that never retreats as t grows is a different kind of result.

The timeline

iii.Three fences

  • 1896 Hadamard & de la Vallée Poussin prove ζ has no zeros on σ = 1 — the key step in the first proof of the prime number theorem. The fence: σ > 1 − c/log t, a curve kissing the line σ = 1.
  • 1958 Vinogradov & Korobov, via exponential sums, widen the fence to σ > 1 − c/((log t)2/3(log log t)1/3). Unbeaten in shape ever since — decades of work (Ford 2002, Mossinghoff–Trudgian 2015) only sharpened the constant.
  • 2026 ✦ Claimed: a manuscript family in the Oct 6, 2026 openai/math release of AI-produced mathematics claims σ > 11/12 ≈ 0.9167, uniformly in t. Not a curve hugging σ = 1 — a wall.
