The n-dimensional story

What you’re seeing

K is a centrally symmetric convex polygon. Its polar K° = { y : x·y ≤ 1 } turns every supporting line of K into a point and every vertex into an edge. Apply the operation twice and you get K back: (K°)° = K — press Swap to feel it.

The 2-D theorem

Mahler, 1939: for every centrally symmetric convex body K ⊂ ℝ²,

area(K)·area(K°) ≥ 8

with equality exactly for parallelograms. The gauge above is the whole story in two dimensions: the product lives between Mahler’s floor of 8 and the Blaschke–Santaló ceiling of π² ≈ 9.87, attained by ellipses. No dragging can break either wall.

The conjecture in ℝⁿ

Mahler’s conjecture: vol(K)·vol(K°) ≥ 4ⁿ/n! The suspected extremals are the cube [−1,1]ⁿ and its polar, the cross-polytope { Σ|xᵢ| ≤ 1 } — a perfect dual pair — plus whole families of “Hanner” mixtures of the two.

The 3-D case fell to Iriyeh & Shibata (2020). From dimension 4 up, it is open — and the crowd of distinct extremals (cubes, cross-polytopes, and their products) is exactly what makes it so hard: no single “round everything out” flow can converge to all of them at once.

Status, honestly

Symmetric case: n=2 settled n=3 settled n≥4 open

The general (non-symmetric) version minimizes over the Santaló point and conjectures ≥ (n+1)ⁿ⁺¹/(n!)², extremal simplex — also open in general.

On Oct 6, 2026, OpenAI published openai/math, a public repo of 722 AI-produced mathematics manuscripts. Family 087 claims progress on the symmetric and general Mahler conjectures. That is a claim, not a theorem — unverified as of today. This page treats it as a lead worth watching, and every number on the left as a theorem worth touching.
