Give it a little nudge.

In perfect-clock mode, add a phase kick: the relationship changes and stays changed. In the restoring-link preset, the mismatch relaxes back toward a stable value. Increase |Δ| beyond K and it keeps winding.

A short observation can miss very slow slips near the boundary. High R₂,₁ alone is never a certificate of coupling.

Inspect the mathematics & numerical assumptions

r(t) = |(eⁱθₛ + eⁱθբ)/2| = |cos((θₛ − θբ)/2)|
R₂,₁ = |⟨eⁱψ⟩ₜ|,   ψ = 2θₛ − θբ

Constructed mode: θₛ = t, θբ = (2 − Δ)t + 0.4, plus the selected kick after halfway. At Δ = 0 and zero kick, R₂,₁ = 1 exactly, while the mean ordinary r over complete cycles is 2/π ≈ 0.63662.

Coupled toy: θ̇ₛ = 1 − (K/4) sin ψ
θ̇բ = 2 − Δ + (K/2) sin ψ
Therefore ψ̇ = Δ − K sin ψ.

This is an explicitly chosen reciprocal resonant phase interaction, not a reduction derived from the source paper or a calibrated material model. The phase-only assumption omits amplitudes, noise and spatial fields. Normalized units have no assigned biological time scale. Extreme controls illustrate the equation beyond any asserted weak-coupling regime.

For K > 0 and |Δ| < K, ψ* = arcsin(Δ/K) is stable (modulo 2π); the other fixed point is unstable. At |Δ| = K > 0 the fixed point is marginal. For |Δ| > K the long-time drift is sgn(Δ)√(Δ² − K²). At Δ = K = 0 every mismatch is neutral, not restored.

RK4 integration of ψ uses Δt ≤ 0.02 over 24π time units. θₛ is recovered from the integrated identity θₛ = t + (ψ − ψ₀ − Δt)/4 between kicks. The kick is applied discontinuously to θբ only. Plots are subsampled; statistics use the integration grid. No inference or simulation runs in the background.

Three ways to look locked without a mutual link

Independent clocks
Exact frequencies chosen by construction can preserve a ratio forever. Perturb one; the other need not respond.

A common driver
Both phases can follow a third source. Recovery alone does not isolate direct coupling; measure or perturb the driver too.

One waveform, two harmonics
x(t) = sin t + 0.3 sin(2t + φ) contains two phase-related spectral components in a single signal. Two peaks need not be two oscillators.

The geometric-phase loop explorer

What comes home
with you?

Move the local phase origins. Individual lags change, but their differences cancel around a closed path. Add a loop phase, and something survives the journey.

Oriented edges 1 → 2 → 3 → 1. Offsets and lags are in radians.

Local offsets, closed pathALGEBRAIC

A change of coordinates

“Absorb” changes both the phase coordinates and the lags. It does not move a physical clock. The edge interaction arguments stay the same. Ordinary global r can change under these node-dependent coordinates.

A physical measurement

A fixed laboratory reference can measure timing offsets. Frequency locking, calibrated edge responses and loop phase modulo 2π are different observables. A nonzero loop phase obstructs satisfying every edge’s zero-lag preference at once; it does not by itself prove a persistent current or a Berry phase.

Follow the signs around the loop

αᵢⱼ = fᵢ − fⱼ + βᵢⱼ,   β₁₂ = β₂₃ = β₃₁ = Φ/3
θ′ᵢ = θᵢ + fᵢ
θⱼ − θᵢ − αᵢⱼ = θ′ⱼ − θ′ᵢ − βᵢⱼ
α₁₂ + α₂₃ + α₃₁ = Φ (mod 2π)

Here an edge i → j labels the argument sin(θⱼ − θᵢ − αᵢⱼ). This is a three-edge algebraic illustration, not a dynamical network simulation. For this single cycle, all lags are removable modulo 2π exactly when the loop sum vanishes modulo 2π. A larger graph requires checking every independent cycle; directed reverse edges can introduce additional constraints.

The transformation removes only node-gradient lags at fixed controls. Coupling amplitudes, other lag terms, and the physical phase reference remain relevant. If fᵢ varies in time, θ̇′ᵢ = θ̇ᵢ + ḟᵢ: the extra term must be included. This page compares static settings; moving a slider does not simulate an evolving geometry.

The fixed illustrative laboratory phases are (0.2, 1.1, −0.5). The coordinate readout uses those phases before and after transformation. A residual loop phase is a graph invariant here, not evidence for quantum transport or gravitational collapse.
