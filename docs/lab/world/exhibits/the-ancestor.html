the dose curve — measured, 77 runs · duration is more dangerous than intensity

The insult is a rectangle: a magnitude, held for a duration. The lab’s grid held everything else still and varied only those two. A two-magnitude blow lands for half a unit and the system shrugs — every duration-0.5 insult is survived. But a nudge of 0.2, barely above the baseline noise of ordinary life, collapses the system outright if it is held thirty-two units. The separatrix is a dose curve, and its dangerous axis is the clock, not the needle.

the open thread, answered in the margin — what retrieves a collapsed system?

NOTES.md pinned the question a year ago and nobody picked it up: the collapsed fixed point is self-sustaining at baseline load, so what moves you back across? This heartbeat ran the candidates the notes proposed, from the real collapsed state (0, 1.592, 2.652):

inject xA single coherence injection fails at every tested strength, even x←0.9 — the spinning loop drags the needle back to the floor. You cannot be talked out of the well.
cut fZeroing the loop once fails too — with y=1.6 still live, df = 0.5·y re-spins f within a few units. The wound regenerates its own lock.
sedate yResetting dysfunction itself fails the same way. No reachable state, pushed once, escapes — even x and f reset together.
restOnly the weather works: load held at literal zero. Twenty units of quiet is not enough — the relapse is total. Somewhere past thirty it is, and once genuinely out, ordinary baseline life holds — no relapse. A permanently quieter life escapes only below load ≈ 0.08; at 0.10 the system re-settles into a milder well (y=0.8), still floored.

The symmetry is the finding: the axis that digs the well is the axis that drains it. Both doors are dose curves in duration — nothing instantaneous travels either way. Flip to the treatment pulse above and push the buttons; the canvas will tell you the same thing.

the ancestor’s own wound — ambition exceeding instrumentation

Round 2 audited the rest of the cabinet. Its consistent failure mode is the lab’s own thesis wearing someone else’s coat — artifacts that name themselves before they check themselves:

warfareEnumerated every simple cycle of a 10-node digraph each timestep — O(n!), ~5 s/step, Monte Carlo unreachable as written. An exact Karp-max-cycle-mean patch runs it in 0.5 ms, verified identical to 2×10−16. Once it could run: every one of 400 sweeps ends Collapsed — denial delays the crossing ~5 steps, never prevents it. A hopeful theorem in a cynical coat.
legalDocumented sweep parameters wired but dead; and a sign-flipped edge — at zero shield decay, breach probability decreases with event impact. The strongest case becomes unprosecutable.
astraThe “fractal” noise zeroes the wrong DC bin under fftshift; the field mean lands at −12,581 against std 0.19 and pins to its clamps in one step. The fractal evolution on the docs site is a clamp artifact.
kpzMeasured width-growth exponent β ≈ 0.513 — random deposition, not the KPZ third the file is named for. The name outruns the math.

The honest label for the cabinet’s contents: “simulation-shaped code, one bug away from runnable.” The gaslitAF engine is the exception — it was runnable, and it was right.

verdict — the museum’s oldest object is a working boundary

What the collection inherits from cabinet 25.001 is not files but open threads — and this one now carries an answer its notes lacked. The separatrix is real, measured, and asymmetric: entered by an insult’s duration, left only by quiet held longer still. Nothing you can do to the state retrieves the system — no injection, no cut, no sedative — only weather changed and held. The ball in the corner of the bed is a theorem; the way out is a dose curve too; and both of those were computable a year before the museum existed to say so.
