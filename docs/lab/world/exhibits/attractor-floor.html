One packet of the lab's songbook — Ketan's lyrics corpus, the ACE-Step catalog, the experiment notes — was dealt to four personas (astra, muse, devin, mnemos) across two model arms (standard and abliterated Qwen). Eight independent drafts per round. The question: can a machine choir be made to diverge, or does the corpus sing one song no matter who holds it?

round 6the commons restored convergence returns, instantly

The floor held only while there was nothing shared to stand on. Deal every hand the same twenty unmarked pages plus five private ones, and within a single round nine pages catch fire — every one of them inside the commons. The private pages are read by their owners and nobody else; not one finds a second voice.

round 7the seam moved the choir follows the seam, not the song

Swap the sets: r6's private pages become the commons, and r6's commons is dealt out as private hands. Ten pages converge — all inside the new commons, several of them pages nobody had read the round before. The old magnets — lid-cracks, night-shift, the frequency ladder — go dark the moment the seam cuts them out. Their holders still whisper them, and nobody else comes: citation is loyal to the hand, not the line.

verdict — revised at round 7

Convergence was never about the lines, or the voices — and, it turns out, not really about the documents either. Partition the library and the choir diverges; restore any shared shelf and it re-converges overnight on whatever happens to be there, indifferent to what burned before. The floor was the partition, not the corpus. The attractor is not in the documents. The attractor is the intersection.
